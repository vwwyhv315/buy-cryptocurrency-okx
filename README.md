## Direct Buy versus spot trading

The difference matters because the two flows are designed for different users.

Direct Buy gives you a quoted transaction. You select an asset and a payment method, review the amount, and confirm. It is straightforward, but you may have less control over the execution price.

Spot trading uses an order book. You choose a trading pair, such as BTC/USD or another available market, and place a market or limit order. A market order generally executes against existing orders and is usually treated as a taker order. A limit order may be treated as a maker order if it rests on the order book, but a limit order that fills immediately can still be charged as a taker order.

That means the order label alone does not determine the fee. The actual execution determines whether the order adds liquidity or removes liquidity.

For a small purchase, the simplicity of Direct Buy may be worth more than the extra control available in spot trading. For repeated purchases or larger orders, comparing the quote against the spot market may be sensible.

## What to check before confirming a purchase

A crypto purchase can look inexpensive until the final preview reveals a different total. Review these items every time:

### 1. The amount you pay

Check whether the displayed amount is in USD, another fiat currency, or an account balance. Card and bank-transfer flows may show different totals for the same target amount of crypto.

### 2. The amount of crypto you receive

Look at the exact crypto quantity rather than only the fiat amount. This lets you compare the quote with the market price and spot-trading alternatives.

### 3. Fees and spread

The purchase preview may show a service fee, payment fee, quote adjustment, or other cost. Direct Buy and Convert quotes can differ from the fee structure used for spot trading.

### 4. The payment method

A method may disappear or become unavailable depending on your verification status, location, chosen asset, purchase size, or payment provider availability. OKX says support cannot manually enable a payment method that is not shown in your account.

### 5. Withdrawal availability

Some purchases may be available for trading before they are eligible for withdrawal. This is especially relevant when buying through bank transfer, ACH, card channels, or P2P.

### 6. Network selection

When withdrawing crypto, choose the network that matches the receiving wallet or platform. Sending an asset through an incompatible network can result in loss of access to the funds. The network fee and minimum withdrawal amount should be checked on the withdrawal screen.

A low maker or taker fee does not necessarily mean the cheapest way to buy. Include spread, card charges, bank-processing costs, network fees, and the price at which the order actually executes.

A purchased balance may appear in the account while withdrawals remain restricted temporarily. Check the order and asset details before planning to move the crypto to an external wallet.

### Sending crypto on the wrong network

The recipient must support the selected network. When in doubt, confirm the receiving platform’s deposit instructions before withdrawing.
